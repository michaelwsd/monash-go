How the test documents fit together

The four levels

Level What it checks A real example from MonashGo Who does it, and when How
Unit One function by itself, with the database and the network faked. The emissions formula gives 2.04 kg of CO2 for a 2024 Camry Hybrid over 18 km. We worked that number out by hand first, so we already know what the right answer looks like. Whoever writes the code. The test comes first, before the code it is testing exists. pytest, with stand-in versions of the database
Integration Several parts working together, through a real request to the API. Booking a seat really does drop the seat count by one, and a request from someone who is not properly signed in gets turned away. The person who built the feature, checked by a Testing Co-ordinator at the end of each sprint. pytest through a test client
System The finished product on the live site, from the browser through to the database. A driver posts a Clayton to Caulfield ride, a passenger finds it and books a seat, and the dashboard then shows all three ways of making that trip. Michael and Raneth, in Sprint 6, once the site is deployed. Working through a written script by hand
Acceptance Whether the product actually does what we said it would. We go through every requirement in the traceability matrix against what we agreed would count as passing, and then let real students use the prototype and tell us what they think. The whole team, plus students who test it for us, at the end of Sprint 6. The matrix as a checklist, and a Google Form survey
